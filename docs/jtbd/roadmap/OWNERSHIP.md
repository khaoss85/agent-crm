# Ownership — who owns each desired job, and what nobody owns

Machine-readable form: `roadmap.overlay.jsonl`, generated by `scripts/jtbd-gate.js --write`
from `capability_pillars.json`, `pillars.json` and `../coverage/matrix_crosswalk.json`.

**This layer states ownership, never coverage and never priority.** A job owned by an
implemented pillar is not thereby supported; a job with a milestone is not thereby built.

## How a job resolves to exactly one owner

1. Each of the catalogue's 225 capabilities is mapped to one Accordo pillar, to `null` (no
   pillar owns it) or to `__out_of_scope__` (this repository declares the capability outside
   the product). The table is `capability_pillars.json` — hand-authored, one line each,
   reviewable as a diff.
2. A job's owner is decided **among owners**: the pillar holding the most of its *core*
   capabilities, ties broken by the declared `precedence` list in `pillars.json`.
3. The unowned fraction is recorded rather than allowed to win. An earlier form let the
   unowned bucket take a plurality, so a renewal job came out "unassigned" because its
   health-score and success-plan halves are unowned — which reads as *nobody owns this* and
   hides the pillar that does. `unownedCoreCapabilities` carries that number instead.
4. `ownershipResolution` is `unassigned_gap` only when **no** core capability has an owner,
   and `out_of_scope` only when none has an owner and at least one is declared out of scope.

`ownerStatus` comes from a different place on purpose: the **crosswalk**. A job is
`in progress` or `planned` only when a matrix row that cites it names a milestone;
`unassigned` otherwise. Ownership never reads the coverage overlay, so a milestone can never
become evidence.

## Unowned jobs

**Zero jobs have no owner at all**, and that number is weaker than it looks. What it says is
that every one of the six hundred has at least one core capability some pillar owns. The
useful number is the next one down.

**60 jobs are majority-unowned**: more than half their core capabilities map to no Accordo
pillar. Those are the honest gaps, and they concentrate in customer-success capabilities the
product has never modelled — health score (`CSV-002`), success plan and outcome tracking
(`CSV-004`), value realization (`CSV-022`), adoption monitoring (`CSV-003`), knowledge base
(`CSV-012`), playbook and coaching (`COL-004`), goal and OKR tracking (`COL-009`).

## Conflicting owners

**558 of 600 jobs name more than one candidate pillar** in their core capabilities: 42 resolve
to a single pillar outright, 142 to two candidates, 228 to three, 171 to four and 17 to five.
The overlay publishes every candidate in `candidatePillars`, in precedence order, so the
resolution can be argued with rather than trusted.

This is not a defect to be tuned away. A business job crosses pillars — that is what makes it
a business job — and a rule that produced one clean owner per job would be hiding the
crossing. What the number *does* mean is that the assigned pillar is a **primary** owner and
never a sole one.

**40 jobs have a milestone belonging to a different pillar than the one they resolve to.** The
largest groups are seven jobs owned by Core CRM whose milestone is M13 (Delivery Handover),
three owned by Analytics whose milestone is M9 (Lead Intelligence), and two owned by Core CRM
whose milestone is M12. This is exactly the boundary between the two derivations: the pillar
is capability-derived, the milestone is crosswalk-derived, and when the matrix row that cites
a job belongs to a different workstream than the job's capabilities do, they disagree
visibly rather than quietly. Both are published; neither overrides the other.

## Pillars with no desired job

Seven of the twenty pillars own **no** canonical job:

| Pillar | Why nothing lands there |
|---|---|
| Signature and Order | one capability maps to it (`SAL-017`), and it never wins a plurality — every job carrying it also carries three or four Commercial capabilities |
| Delivery | three capabilities map to it and none is a first-class job for any of the thirty personas |
| Work | five capabilities, always outnumbered by the domain capabilities beside them |
| Customer Data Operations | five capabilities — bulk, import/export, search, dedupe, timeline — spread thinly across administrator and marketing-operations jobs |
| Interactions | six capabilities, never a plurality |
| Billing | two capabilities, and the catalogue models billing as *consumed data* rather than as work anyone does |
| Design-to-CRM | one capability (`COL-007`) |

Read carefully, this is a statement about the **catalogue**, not about Accordo. Delivery,
Work, Signature and Contracts are among the most heavily built parts of this framework;
they own few desired jobs because the corpus was written around commercial roles.

## Milestones with no desired job

29 milestones named in `pillars.json` reach no canonical job through the crosswalk, including
every Cloud milestone (`C0`–`C6`), every Spine milestone after v1, every DX milestone,
`Customer Data Operations v2`, `Interactions package`, `Billing domain`, `DATA_GOVERNANCE.md`
and `DESIGN_TO_CRM.md`. Five of them (`C0`, `DX3`, `M14b2`, `M5`, `M7`) *are* named by a
crosswalk row and still reach no job, because every row naming them is one of the unmatched
rows above.

Twenty of the catalogue's 225 capabilities are referenced by **no** record, core or
supporting. They are the reverse gap — capability vocabulary with no job behind it:

communication · `CSV-018` entitlement, contract and service eligibility · `DAT-008` golden
record and MDM · `DEV-006` custom action and serverless function · `DEV-018` versioned
migration · `GOV-001` SSO and MFA · `GOV-004` DSAR, export and delete · `GOV-012` vendor and
connector governance · `GOV-015` access review and recertification · `GOV-016` data
minimization and purpose limitation · `MKT-008` web and in-app personalization ·
`PLT-004` custom object and custom field · `PLT-009` search, indexing and views ·
`PLT-014` localization, currency and timezone · `PLT-015` mobile and offline ·
`SAL-023` mobile sales workspace.

Three deserve naming out loud:

- **`PLT-004` custom object and custom field is unreferenced.** It is the single most-proven
  capability in this repository — `JTBD-01` and `JTBD-01b` are the oldest *validated end to
  end* rows in the matrix — and not one of the six hundred desired jobs names it. The corpus
  assumes a CRM that already has its objects.
- **`AUT-005` scheduled and recurring job is unreferenced**, while `EXECUTION_ROADMAP.md`
  records that jobs and outbox sit on the critical path for more JTBDs than any single domain
  milestone. The dependency is real and the corpus does not see it.
- **`GOV-001` SSO and MFA is unreferenced**, and authentication is this repository's single
  largest declared gap (`spine.authentication.framework_verifier=absent`).

## No orphans, and what that is worth

Every job that is `not supported` or `partially supported` has a milestone **or** an explicit
`unassigned` / `deferred` / `out of scope` status; `JTBD_ROADMAP_ORPHAN` never fires. That is
true by construction rather than by achievement: `ownerStatus` is `unassigned` exactly when no
milestone claims the job, so the rule cannot fail. What it buys is that the number is
published and cannot be quietly skipped — **503 of 600 desired jobs are claimed by no
milestone**, and that is the generated fact `jtbd.roadmap.unassigned_count`.

## Do not use the raw count as a progress metric

97 jobs carry a milestone and 503 do not. Neither number measures the framework. The corpus
was written by somebody with a view of the market; its size, its shape and its persona mix are
properties of that document. Moving 503 towards 0 by writing more crosswalk rows would change
the number and nothing else. The only figures here that describe the framework are the five
coverage rows with evidence attached, and `docs/QUALITY_GATES.md` §3 governs those.
